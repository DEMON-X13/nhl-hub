# The map

Deeper reference than `CLAUDE.md`. Read that first for the loop and the working agreement; read
this when you need to find something. It is a map, not a copy of the code -- the code is the truth,
and anything here that contradicts it is out of date.

## What this is

One site, X NHL Bets, served straight from the repo root by GitHub Pages at
https://demon-x13.github.io/nhl-hub/. No server, no framework, no bundler. The page carries no
season data: it fetches `state.json`, which the scheduled job commits, on every load. One scheduled
GitHub Actions job, `.github/workflows/update.yml`, rebuilds the data from ESPN's public feeds and
DailyFaceoff and commits it. It began as `nhl/` in `DEMON-X13/nfl-hub` and moved here whole on
2026-10-13; the old address, `nfl-hub/nhl/`, redirects here. The history before the move is
nfl-hub's: `git log 3bc336d -- nhl/ .github/workflows/nhl.yml` in `DEMON-X13/nfl-hub`.

The parlays a visitor builds and saves stay in that browser's local storage (`nhl_v1`, with their stake
and their picks on the Parlay Builder). Nothing on the page writes anywhere but the visitor's own browser;
everything else changes only with a commit.

## The site: the NBA Hub's idea, on nfl-hub's shape

X NHL Bets is the shape of nfl-hub's college site (one page, one job, one state file) with the
NBA Hub's unit and look: the NHL has no weeks, so the page opens on a day strip, yesterday to
ten days out, one card per game. Moneylines, puck lines and totals; and a player props model, on trial
(below), shown beside DraftKings' own player prices and never suggested. Everything is ESPN's public feeds,
free.

The job reads every day of the season (skipping the days already complete in the last state; a
day through tomorrow with a game to finish or call must answer, or the run exits 1 having written
nothing), replays the finished seasons and this season's finals in date order, and gives every
game still to come the model's chance, expected margin and total, the overtime chance, and
DraftKings' moneyline, puck line and total. That call is frozen at puck drop by the clock (a game
ESPN still has as scheduled past its start, or has moved less than half a day later after it, is not
called again, and the smoke test holds it as under way, not to this run's lineups) and every part of
it is copied after, so the record grades what the page showed; rows an older job recomputed after puck drop are
put back once to the call shown. The job runs `update.js` twice: the first pass (`--scores`) reads the
same days, writes the scores and the schedule and copies every call as it was, so `fetch_box.js` boxes
last night's finals and `players.js` lines up on the schedule the second pass calls on; only the second
makes calls. A game comes off the schedule only on the feed's word (postponed, cancelled, or moved to
another day); one an answer leaves out is kept with its call, line and grade (`kept`), a blank answer
on a day the last state has games on is no answer (unless the answer has each of them on another day,
the same id: they moved), and what came off is in `removed` with the reason. A side is taken only where the model's chance beats the book's
implied by five points; the record counts straight-up calls on every game and the sides taken on
each market. The playoff picture, in the regular season, is two thousand simulations of the rest
of it (overtime and its loser point from the goals layer), the league's bracket (top three a
division, two wild cards a conference), four best-of-seven rounds with home ice to the higher seed;
once the regular season is over the field is the real one and each series ESPN has a game in is
played on from its real score, so a club knocked out has no chance left and the bracket shows every
series' score (`phase`: preseason, regular, postseason, over, offseason). The field is then the
sixteen clubs of ESPN's first round, its seeds and wild cards read off the bracket: the table breaks
ties only to goal difference, so a club it left out that ESPN has in a series is in for certain, and
the club it displaced is out.

WHO PLAYS (`players.js`): each game's lineup is who dressed last less anyone ESPN's injury report has
out, on injured reserve or suspended, or lists under another club; the goalie, game by game, is
DailyFaceoff's for that game and date, else the club's most frequent starter in its last ten games
this season, the other goalie on the second night of a back to back (other than the box score's
starter of the night before, or the one expected for it while it is not boxed). The page's clock decides what
has started: the Games tab's Today is the visitor's Eastern date, a game past its puck drop offers no
bet and says it is under way, a live score the job published says when it was read (and the page
reads ESPN itself once on opening for a game under way), and a leg whose game has started leaves the
builder. Tabs: Games, Parlay Builder (`#parlays`, `#parlay` too; browser only, no sync), Standings, Power
Ratings, Playoff, Players (the player model's rankings and the sources it stands on), Record.

REFRESH ODDS (nhl v9, `index.html`; CLAUDE.md has the rules): a button on the Games toolbar and under the Parlay
Builder panel's heading reads ESPN's scoreboard in the browser, on a press and never else, for the strip's days
from today with a game still to come. `ESPN` in the page is `tools/espn.js`'s `oddsOf`/`dkOf`/`carryDk` and `MODEL`
is `tools/elo.js`'s goals layer, each word for word (`carryDk`, the carry rule, is the job's too: `update.js` reads
every line through it); `oddsCall` is the line half of `update.js`'s `callOf`. A game still to come by the clock and
by ESPN's word takes the new line (marked `refreshed`, so it is fresh) and the call's edges, sides and chances
follow; one ESPN has under way, over, postponed or cancelled keeps its line, counted in the status line. Everything
drawn from the prices redraws: the levels keep their locks and New legs (`pbCarry`), the builder draws on the new
lines while its stored legs stay as they were, an open card follows. In memory only: nothing is stored, and a
reload is the published state. The same press reads DraftKings' player props for the games today and tomorrow
(THE PROPS VIEW, below).

THE PARLAY BUILDER (nhl v7, `index.html`, every class `pb-` for the panel and `pc-` for the card; CLAUDE.md
has the rules): the panel (`#pbPanel`: Bet amount, Build from, Bet types, Day, Games, the notes, four levels
found by exact search and the rules folded under them), Pick your own legs (`#plLines`), the builder
(`#builder`, re-read from the state on every draw), the saved parlays (`#savedList`), the slip bar (`#slip`)
and the card window (`#pcWin`). Its numbers are the state's alone: a leg is the game row's own chance, edge
and prices, offered only on a fresh line (`line.at` is `published`) and a game not started by the visitor's
clock; one leg a game, so a parlay's chance and price are products. In the state each line may carry
`dk: {event, homeML, awayML, homePL, awayPL, over, under}` (and `at` only on outcome ids carried from an
earlier read, when an answer gave no outcome id), taken with the line and frozen with it. A line's `book` is
ESPN's provider name; anything but DraftKings (any case or spacing) is named on the page and given no link. Every
time on the page is the visitor's own with its zone named; the card, its image and its Share text are Eastern
and say ET. A saved leg on a game the state lists as called off has the void written into it (`void`), since
`removed` names a game for one publish only. The smoke's THE PARLAY BUILDER section holds all of it
with its own code, and its REFRESH ODDS section the page's own read of ESPN. `sources.js` counts
DraftKings' ids by outcome (each side of each market), so one market whose ids stop reading fails the run
though the rest read.

`.github/workflows/update.yml` runs five times a day, each slot queued for the window it must land in
at the 2.7 to 8.7 hours late GitHub started this job's runs in `nfl-hub` (October 2026; this repo's
own lateness is not measured yet), and once on a push to `tools/`, the page or the workflow:
`simulate.js`, `update.js --scores`,
`fetch_box.js`, `starters.js`, `players.js`, `props.js`, `update.js`, `smoke.js`, the commit (`state.json`,
`data/teams.json`, `data/box_*.jsonl`, `data/injuries.json`, `data/starters.json`,
`data/players.json`, `data/props.json`, `data/props_ledger.json`, `data/props_*.jsonl` and any
`data/season_*.json`), then `sources.js`, which fails the run after the commit when a source the state
stands on broke (the closing props among them: every final asked for failing).

THE PROPS MODEL (`tools/props.js`, on trial; CLAUDE.md has the rules, the tests and the numbers): after `players.js`
the job's `props.js` step (1) reads the closing props of every final the record lacks (one ESPN request a game, at
most 40 a run) into `data/props_<season>.jsonl`; (2) walks every box score for each player's decayed history and
projects every game still to come through tomorrow (Eastern) on players.js's lineup and goalie for it and its expected
shots and goals each side, a mean and a whole distribution for each expected skater's goals, assists, points, shots
and blocked shots and each goalie's saves; (3) freezes the ledger's calls whose puck drop has passed (the job's own
started rule, delays included), grades each frozen call once its game is final, boxed and recorded, and writes a call
for each game projected (rewritten while it changes, never after its puck drop); (4) writes `data/props.json`, which
`update.js` copies into the state as `props` for the games still to come by its own clock. The constants are
`data/props_model.json`'s (source), never refitted by the job. The smoke's THE PROPS MODEL section holds the box files'
new columns, the record's shape, every distribution and projected player, the state's projections against the
ledger's open calls, and runs `props_check.js` on the ledger against the last commit's.

THE PROPS VIEW (nhl v10, `index.html`, every class `pp-`; CLAUDE.md has the rules and the tests): a Player props button
on each game card still to come that the props model projects (and in Pick your own legs) opens a window over the page,
six stats, each club apart. Before a press: the model's projections (`state.props`). The Refresh odds press reads ESPN's
DraftKings propBets for every game still to come today and tomorrow beside the scoreboards (`readPropsGame`, one request
a game, a second page only where paged); `PROPS_FEED` is `tools/espn.js`'s `propsOf` and `pOver` is `tools/props.js`'s,
each word for word; `ppTake` keeps each game's answer in `PROPS` (200, none posted yet, or failed, which keeps an earlier
read), never a game started by the clock or at ESPN. After it each priced player's row is DraftKings' line and prices,
then "projection · model · DraftKings" (the model's chance of the Over and DraftKings' fair chance, all of the margin on
the Over), then the milestones at their Yes prices (fair chance est.). The model is labelled on trial with the ledger's
running numbers, suggests nothing and never says "model says" or "edge"; nothing is stored, and a reload is the
published page. The smoke's PLAYER PROPS section holds all of it on answers it makes from the state's projections.

## Gotchas

- Editing a generated file is wasted work: the next scheduled run overwrites it. Change the source
  and let the job (or a push to `tools/`, which runs it) rebuild it.
- `data/history.json` and `data/model.json` look generated but are **source**: each changes only
  with a deliberate pull or refit, committed with it.
- The smoke test and the simulation need `npm ci` in `tools/` first (jsdom).
- With no commit to read (`git show HEAD:state.json` fails) or with `NHL_STATE` set and no
  `NHL_PREV_STATE`, the smoke test has no last published state, and the checks against it are
  skipped, not failed.
- DailyFaceoff answers GitHub's runners but not every sandbox; `starters.js` failing to read it
  locally is said in `data/starters.json`, not a fault in the code.
- The page's origin, `demon-x13.github.io`, is shared with the owner's other Pages sites, so its
  local storage key `nhl_v1` must stay its own.
- `data/props_model.json` looks generated but is **source** (`node tools/props.js fit`, finished seasons
  only). A refit changes its sha, and the props ledger then makes no new call until its bar is decided:
  the trial is of the constants it registered. `data/props_ledger.json` and `data/props_<season>.jsonl` are
  records the job keeps; `props_check.js` (in the smoke) fails a ledger whose frozen calls or grades moved.
- The props ledger grows about 6 KB a game graded (each frozen call's means and its graded lines), some
  8 MB over a full season; the record of closing props about 5 KB a final.
